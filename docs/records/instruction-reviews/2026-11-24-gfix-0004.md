# Instruction review: docs/decisions/0004-the-hook-contract.md (slice GFIX)

This records a fresh session's narrow re-check of slice GFIX's code and its edit to decision 0004, for the slice's builder and the maintainer.

**In short:** 7 findings: 4 enforcement, 3 contradiction; 2 are blocking. A NUL escape at the start of a word, such as `$'\0'git push --force origin main`, passes all seven shell guards, and bash runs git. A wrapper named in another letter case, such as `NICE -n 5 git push -f origin main`, passes all seven, and both shells run git. The ANSI-C, arithmetic, wrapper-word, depth and case routes hold in their direct variants otherwise, and no fixed program list is left. Of the 1,539 stored commands, 9 are newly refused, all `type git` or `which -a git` in one eval case. The re-check of fix round 1 found findings 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 closed as found, and 3 new items, 2 of them blocking direct variants of finding 1 that pass all seven guards, on 2d83108 too: an octal escape above `\377`, such as `$'\547it' push --force origin main`, and a word of only a NUL escape among `eval`'s words, such as `eval $'\0' '${G:-git}' push --force origin main`. The re-check of fix round 2 found items 8, 9 and 10 closed as found, and 5 new items, 3 of them blocking escape spellings the rule reads otherwise than a shell does, each passing all seven guards, on 6efbff3 too: zsh's NUL from `\x`, `\u` or `\U` with no digit, such as `git$'\x' push --force origin main`; bash's `\x{...}`, such as `eval $'echo x\x{3b}git push --force origin main'`; and `\c` or `\C-` before another escape, such as `eval $'\C-\x3bgit push --force origin main'`.
