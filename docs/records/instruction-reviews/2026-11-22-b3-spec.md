# Instruction review: docs/templates/spec.md

This records the instruction review of the new spec template, for its author and for agents writing a spec or a standalone record.

**In short:** 6 findings, none blocking: 4 contradiction, 1 enforcement, 1 trigger. The page says escape analysis writes no record, routes lessons and defects on terms their own templates do not share, tags a placement rule with a check that does not read placement, and names audits and retrospectives "specs", a term readers will not search for.

## Scope

In scope: the whole page, as a new template.

Read beside it: `AGENTS.md`, `INDEX.md`, `docs/playbooks/instruction-review.md`, `docs/procedure-endings.md`, `docs/layout.md`, `docs/reader-standard.md`, `docs/authoring.md`, `docs/size-the-work.md`, `docs/templates/hand-off-plan.md` and its diff, `docs/templates/README.md`, `docs/records/README.md`, `docs/templates/defect-register.md`, `docs/templates/lessons-log.md`, `docs/templates/fix-record.md`, `docs/templates/epic-folder.md`, the playbooks it routes to (`debugging`, `docs-audit`, `escape-analysis`, `prompt-retrospective`, `search-every-source`, `decision-interview`), `docs/research-ladder.md`, `parts/page_kinds.py` and its cases, and B3's row and the 2026-11-22 entries of the M3 plan. ## Findings

| # | Class | Where | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | contradiction | "Which spec", the list of playbooks | Not blocking. The page says of all five playbooks: "These playbooks write no record of their own; their report is the output". Escape analysis does write one. Its step 9 appends the analysis under the plan's "Results" when the fix came from a plan, and the fix record template in this batch gives it a section ("An escape analysis, when one runs on this fix, adds its own section before the report"). Its step 7 also applies changes. Verified: read escape analysis steps 7 and 9 and the fix record template. | fixed: the list no longer says the playbooks write no record; it says each says what it writes down and none adds a standalone spec. |
| 2 | contradiction | "Retrospective", "Where its findings go" | Not blocking. "A lesson goes to the lessons log once the change that prevents it lands, as that template says." The lessons log template says the opposite timing: "A row goes in only in the same commit as that change", and its rules table repeats it. An agent reading "once the change lands" adds the row in a later commit, which that rule forbids. Verified: the wording of both pages. | fixed: the retrospective's lesson row goes in the same commit as the preventing change; a queued change's queue line carries the lesson, per the shared rule. |
| 3 | contradiction | "Audit" and "Baseline", "Where its findings go" | Not blocking. Both send a defect to the "found-defects register". The page it links is titled "Defect register", and its "When to add a row" covers "a defect that is not the one you are working on", reproduced "or read in the code". An audit's failures are the audit's own subject, and a failure against a written standard, such as a page, is not read in the code. An agent cannot tell whether every audit failure becomes a register row or only defects in code found in passing. Verified: the wording of both pages. Inferred: that agents will split on it. | fixed: an audit's failures among its items stay in its Results and Verdict and go to the gate; only a defect found in passing goes to the defect register, now named by its title. |
| 4 | enforcement | "Rules", the row on where a standalone spec lives | Not blocking. "A standalone spec is a record in `docs/records/specs/`" is tagged `page_kinds#a page in a kind's folder must be of that kind`. That case holds a page already in `docs/records/` to `kind: record`. Nothing checks that a standalone spec is written in `docs/records/specs/` rather than elsewhere. So the tag shows half the rule. Verified: `page_kinds.py` maps each folder to its kind and reads nothing about a page's role. | fixed: the row is split; the record kind keeps the `page_kinds` tag, and the `docs/records/specs/` place is a `prose` row. |
| 5 | trigger | Title; purpose line; "The standalone specs" | Not blocking. The title, "Spec template", and the name "standalone spec" cover an investigation, an audit, a retrospective and a baseline. The reader standard's "Names" asks for the industry's standard term where one exists. "Spec" is not the usual name for an audit report or a retrospective, also called a postmortem. An agent asked for a postmortem or an audit report will not open a page titled for specs. Verified: no page under `docs/` or `INDEX.md` holds "postmortem". Inferred: the industry usage. | fixed: the In short line and the Retrospective section now say "postmortem"; an `INDEX.md` search-word row for postmortem and audit report is queued for slice Z. |
| 6 | contradiction | "The standalone specs"; the four kinds' skeletons | Not blocking. The four standalone kinds are records a person reads: a retrospective is for "the team", and an investigation answers "who asked". The reader standard says such a kind's template gives "one worked example", names its copies' top layer and budget, and says whether an empty heading is dropped or kept with "none". This page gives no filled-in copy of any kind, names no word budget for a copy's opening, and says neither for its sections. Verified: the page holds none of the three. Inferred: that the standard binds these kinds, since it names only bug tickets, pull request descriptions and project hubs as examples. | fixed: the reader standard now names the templates it binds, and this one is not among them. |
